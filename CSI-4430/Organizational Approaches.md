# Systems View
A holistic and analytical approach to management and problem solving. 
- Philosophy: an overall model for thinking about things as systems
- Analysis: problem-solving approach
- Management: address business, technological, and organizational issues before making changes to systems

A systems view requires that project managers always view their projects in the context of the larger organization. 
# The Four Frames of Organization

| Structural frame: Roles and responsibilities, coordination, and control. Organizational charts help describe this frame. | HR frame: Providing harmony between needs of the organization and needs of people.                                               |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Political frame**: Coalitions composed of varied individuals. Conflict and Power are key issues.                       | **Symbolic frame**: Symbols and meanings related to events. Culture, language, traditions, and image are all part of this frame. |
# Culture
Ten characteristics of organization culture:
- Member identity
- Group emphasis
- People focus
- Unit integration
- Control
- Risk tolerance
- Reward criteria
- Conflict tolerance
- Means-ends orientation
- Open-systems focus
Project work is most successful in an organizational culture where these items are important. 
# IT Governance
This addresses the authority and control for key IT activities in organizations, including IT infrastructure, IT use, and project management. 

A few IT governance failures are:
- Sydney Water's customer relationship management system
- The Royal Melbourne Institute of Technology's academic management system
- One.Tel's billing system
## Organizational Commitment to IT
- If the organization has a negative attitude toward IT, it will be difficult for an IT project to succeed. 
- Having a CIO at a high level in the organization helps IT projects.
- Assigning non-IT people to IT projects also encourages more commitment.
